In 1909, the most famous tower on Earth was scheduled to be torn down. You see, when Gustave Eiffel built it for the 1889 World's Fair, Paris hated it. The country's most famous artist signed a letter calling it, Useless and Monstrous. The writer Guy de Maupassant supposedly ate lunch in its restaurant every single day. Why? It was the only place in Paris where he couldn't see the tower. And the deal was simple. After 20 years, the city would take it back and it could come down. So Eiffel needed a reason to keep it up. He let scientists bolt weather instruments to it. He let them drop things off it. Then he found the winner, Radio. The tower was the tallest antenna in the world. Luckily, the army loved it. Unluckily for Germany, so did everyone listening. In 1914, its transmitter jammed German radio during the Battle of the Marne and the army marching on Paris was stopped. Later, it caught a coded message about a spy called H -21. That spy was Mata Hari. The tower that was supposed to come down ended up protecting the city that wanted it gone. Today, it gets 60 tons of fresh paint every seven years. And if you'd rather look at it than eat lunch inside it, this one comes ready to frame.
