In 1990, engineers closed the Leaning Tower of Pisa because it was falling over. Their job was to fix it without fixing it. You see, the tower started leaning in 1178 when the builders had only reached the second floor. The ground underneath was soft clay and sand. It's like building on a wet sponge. Then Pisa went to war with Genoa, with Luca, with Florence, and construction stopped for almost a hundred years. Luckily, that war saved the tower. While nobody was building, the soil slowly packed down and got stronger. Unluckily, when the builders came back, they didn't start over. They tried to fix the lean by making one side of each new floor taller. So the tower isn't just leaning, it's curved like a banana. By 1990, it leaned five and a half degrees. So engineers stacked 870 tons of lead on the high side. Then they pulled 38 cubic meters of soil out from under it, a little at a time. The tower slowly settled back 45 centimeters to how it leaned in 1838. And then they stopped, on purpose. Because a straight leaning tower of Pisa is just a tower. It reopened in 2001, and engineers say it should hold for another 200 years. And if you want one that stays at exactly this angle forever, it comes in a frame.
