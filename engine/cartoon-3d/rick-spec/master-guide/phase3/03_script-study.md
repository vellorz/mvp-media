## Hook types and first-3-seconds pattern
- **A. Strange act already happening** (subject + past-tense verb + odd object, 9-21 words): 01, 02, 03, 04, 05, 08, 10, 11, 13, 14, 16, 17 = **12/20** (all 6 top videos except 12/19 use A or B).
  e.g. "He put a bronze ball in a dragon's mouth and told the emperor if this ever falls out, run." (#14 0-3.1 s); "They buried a man alive with a bell tied to his finger." (#10 0-2.9 s).
- **B. Impossible rule / paradox**: 06, 07, 12, 15, 19 = 5/20 ("Gambling is illegal in Japan. Yet, there's a gambling hall on almost every street." #19).
- **C. Direct address / question**: 09, 18, 20 = 3/20 (weakest views except 09).
- First 3 s: 9-12 words spoken (3.4-4.1 w/s from frame 1, no intro, no silence; first word at t=0.0-0.2 s in all 20). The picture shows subject + object + the person who will doubt him by 2.4 s (#14 f71).
- Hook never contains a date, a place name first, or "Did you know". Dates appear only inside the story (#10 "2:00 in the morning").

## Beat order (story videos, 16/20)
1. Hook — strange act (0-3 s).
2. "You see," context — the world rule that makes it make sense (3-8 s; "You see" in 15/20 videos, first use at 2.4-7.2 s in 9 of them).
3. Doubter — rival, boss, captain, crowd, court, police laughs/mocks, usually with a quoted line ("Barbie's going to love it in here." #02; "Did your frogs get hungry again?" #14). Quoted lines in 13/20.
4. Escalation 2-3 times with time markers ("Weeks later", "Then, one night", "In May... By July", "By the fifth", "At 15 minutes... At 20...").
5. Turn at 55-75 % ("But then", "Until one night", "Wait, what?", "Then nobody moved").
6. Reveal / mechanism starts at 50-85 % of runtime (measured on all 20 with an anchor phrase per video: 65,65,55,66,71,74,65,84,50,59,65,70,82,71,77,73,73,44,76,55 % → median 68 %; 19/20 inside 50-85 %, only the #18 explainer earlier), usually opened by "You see," or "Wait, what?". Mechanism explained by an everyday analogy (soup, phone slipping off a table, car on a hairpin, smoke alarms).
7. Payoff on the LAST word; the antagonist gets comeuppance or the twist lands ("...and the house was all hers." #10; "...right underneath them." #14). No moral, no CTA, no "follow for more".
- Explainer/list videos (15, 18, 20) skip 3-5 and rank lower in views.

## Narration pacing (measured, all 20)
- Words/s: median **3.75** (3.38-4.13). Every video ≥ 3.38.
- Max gap between word starts: median 0.76 s (0.60-1.16); 18/20 ≤ 0.88 s. Median gap 0.24 s.
- Sentence length: mean 15 words (11.8-24.2), mixing long run-ons (up to 49) with 2-5 word punches ("Not really." "Then another." "Wait, practiced?"). 
- Runtime 56-65 s for 15/20; 4 at 31-45 s; one 78 s (lowest-viewed story).
- Pitch (mixed track, praat): #10 167 Hz, #14 171 Hz, #07 190 Hz, #12 182 Hz; p5-p95 range 18-20.5 semitones. Laptop stem study (01-05): 197-211 Hz, 12.7-14.7 st on the isolated voice.
- Register: casual spoken American ("guy", "five bucks", "Bro", "Pal"), one joke or sight-gag line every ~10-15 s, numbers as concrete specifics (39 ft, 300 lb, 15 minutes, 47 scratches) — 1-4 per video, never a list of statistics.
- Emphasis habits: rhetorical "Wait, what?" / "Does he know what he's doing? Not really."; contrast "But"; "So," to advance (up to 7 per video); character voices for quoted lines by the same narrator.

## Ending and poster
Rick ends 0.27-0.57 s after the final word starts (#14 8 f, #10 17 f), on the payoff image, mid camera move; 3/20 end mid-sentence so the last line loops into the first (02, 03, 04). Rick never shows a product. Our poster ending therefore has no direct Rick evidence; the guide derives it from Rick's ending rule (see guide section g, marked [derived]).
